The correction path at /legal/corrections (SEO-RULES §3.8; GATE 50/54), the companion to GW-017. It defines what counts as a factual error, the two real ways to report one, what happens next — with an outcome promised and no clock promised — and how a fix is shown on the corrected page itself. Corrections policy

If something SalamStay published is factually wrong, tell us and we'll fix the page. Corrections are made on the page itself, marked with what changed, and listed in the log at the bottom of this page. We don't quietly rewrite a fact and leave it looking like it always said that.

Effective 24 July 2026 Last reviewed 24 July 2026

Definition

What counts as a correction

A correction is a factual error on a page we published — something a reader could reasonably have relied on that wasn't true.

  • A wrong local factA misnamed market or masjid, a distance or walking time that isn't right, an area described as something it isn't.
  • A document rule that doesn't match the appA page saying one document is needed for a booking type when the product actually asks for another.
  • A misnamed or wrong authorityThe wrong body named for a registration or licence rule, or a rule attributed to an authority that doesn't set it.
  • A rule that changed and a page that didn'tA stale fact is still a wrong fact. If the world moved and our page didn't, that is ours to correct.

What isn't a correction

  • A host updating their own listingA host changing their price, rules, photos or availability is an update to their home, not an error we made. It isn't logged here.
  • A typo or a broken linkFixed as soon as we see it, without a log entry — nothing about the meaning changed. If a typo did change a fact, it's a correction and it's logged.
  • A disagreement with a policy"This rule shouldn't exist" is feedback, and welcome — but the page describing the rule accurately isn't an error. Write to us anyway.
  • Something a guest or host wroteReviews and host descriptions are their authors' words. If one breaks the community standards it's handled there, through reporting, not through this log.

Reporting

How to tell us something is wrong

Two paths, both real, both read by a person. Tell us the page and the sentence if you can — it's the fastest way to get it fixed, but a rough description is fine too.

  • Contact support about a pageFor anything SalamStay wrote — a city page, an area page, a help article, a policy. Write in Urdu or English. A person reads every ticket.
  • Report something on a listingFor what a host wrote about their own home, or anything that breaks the community standards. Reporting is private — the person you report is never told who reported them.

Process

What happens after you report one

Four steps. We promise you an outcome and an answer — we don't promise you a deadline, because we don't publish one anywhere else either.

  1. We check the sentence against its source Every factual page keeps a record of where each fact came from. The first thing we do is open that record, not argue from memory.
  2. If we were wrong, the page is corrected The fact is replaced with the right one — or, if we can't establish the right one, the sentence is removed. A page that shrinks is better than a page that guesses.
  3. The correction is marked on the page and logged A dated note goes at the foot of the corrected page saying what changed, the page's last-reviewed date moves, and a row is added to the log below.
  4. You get an answer either way Including when we conclude the page was right — in which case we tell you what the source was, so you can push back with a better one.
If an error affects a booking, it doesn't wait for this process Anything that changes what you can book, what you'll pay or what document you need is handled through support straight away, on your booking. The page fix and the log entry follow after.

On the page

How a correction appears where you found the error

The log is the index; the correction itself lives on the corrected page, so a reader who never comes here still sees that something changed.

The note added to a corrected page
Correction

{Date} — this page previously said {what it said}. It now says {what it says}. {One line on what the correct source was.}

Braces are placeholders showing the shape of the note. Every corrected page carries one, in both languages, at the foot of the page — never hidden behind a link.
