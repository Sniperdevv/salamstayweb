How cantonment rules work

SE SalamStay Editorial Last reviewed 24 July 2026 · Available in Urdu
In short

A cantonment is a military-administered zone. Pakistan has several in and around its cities, each with its own rules for visitors from abroad. A stay inside one can't be booked by guests visiting from abroad until the host's No-Objection Certificate (NOC) is verified. Pakistani guests can book a cantonment stay either way, and nothing about the process changes for them.

What a cantonment is

A cantonment is a military-administered zone. Pakistan has several in and around its cities, each with its own rules for visitors from abroad — Rawalpindi Cantonment is one you'll see named on listings in Rawalpindi.

Cantonment areas have additional rules for foreign guests, set by local authorities. They are ordinary residential areas to live in and to visit; what's different is the paperwork that applies when a guest is travelling on a foreign passport. Homes inside one are listed on SalamStay like any other home, with the same photos, prices and cultural attributes.

If you're booking a stay

If you're booking with a Pakistani CNIC, a cantonment stay behaves exactly like any other stay — you'll see the same verification steps and the same price breakdown.

If you're visiting from abroad and the home you've opened is inside a cantonment, SalamStay tells you before you get to checkout, on the listing itself. This is a local rule for the area — not the host's choice, and not about your profile. It doesn't affect any of your other bookings, and the same home may become bookable later if the host's NOC is verified.

Who can book a home inside a cantonment
Guest What applies
Guests booking with a Pakistani CNIC Bookings open — nothing changes for them.
Guests visiting from abroad Off until the host's NOC is verified. When it is, international bookings open on that listing.
You are not stuck Homes in non-restricted sectors nearby are shown to you the moment a cantonment stay is blocked — you don't have to work out which areas those are. Browse stays in Rawalpindi or stays in Islamabad to see what's open to you.

If you're hosting in a cantonment

When a listing's address falls inside a cantonment, SalamStay matches it to the specific area — "Rawalpindi Cantonment, Rawalpindi, Punjab", for example — and adds one document to your checklist. A local rule, set by local authorities, requires a No-Objection Certificate (NOC) before you can host guests visiting from abroad.

This isn't about your listing or your hosting. It's the same rule guests see when they view a cantonment stay, written from the other side. Pakistani guests can book your home the whole time — before you upload anything, while it's being reviewed, and after. The NOC opens international bookings; it doesn't unlock your listing.

Adding your NOC, step by step

  1. Open the cantonment NOC step in your host checklist It appears on its own once your address is matched to a cantonment — you don't have to go looking for it, and it doesn't appear at all for listings outside one.
  2. Add the document A clear photo or a PDF both work. You can take a photo there and then, or upload a file you already have.
  3. Add the NOC reference number, if you have it to hand This one is optional. It helps us match your document faster, but a missing reference never blocks the upload.
  4. Submit it and carry on hosting Your NOC is reviewed by our team, not automatically. Pakistani guests can keep booking while it's in review, and you'll be notified as soon as it's verified.
  5. International bookings open Once it's verified, guests visiting from abroad can book your home, and your listing shows the verified date alongside the reference you gave us.
CNIC-verified guests and hosts via NADRA Verisys The cantonment NOC sits on top of that check, for one listing. It doesn't replace it, and it doesn't apply to any of your other homes.
