Editorial and fact-check policy

This policy says what SalamStay is allowed to put on a page and how each fact gets there. Every claim comes from one closed list. Every local fact — a named area, a distance, a document rule — is traced to a source before the page publishes. Anything that can't be traced doesn't ship: the sentence goes, not the source.

Effective 24 July 2026 Last reviewed 24 July 2026

Scope

What this policy covers

It covers everything SalamStay writes: city and area pages, help articles, guides, the trust pages, the wording inside the app, and the descriptions that end up in search results. It applies in Urdu exactly as it applies in English — the two versions carry the same facts in the same words, and a page whose Urdu is not real translation is held back rather than published thin.

Two things it does not cover. Legal contracts — the terms, the privacy policy, the guest refund policy and the host terms — are drafted as agreements, not as editorial, and say so in their own text. And what a host writes about their own home is theirs: we normalise a listing title so it names its area and stays one topic, and we remove copy that breaks the community standards, but the description of a home is the host's account of it, shown as the host's, not as our finding.

What we may say

SalamStay makes nine claims, and no others

There is a single approved list of nine claims — verification, alcohol policy, women-only stays, cultural attributes, load-shedding and backup power, reviews and support, and fee transparency. The list is printed in full on our about page, in the exact words every page has to use.

Three rules hold it together. The claims are used verbatim — a writer may not rephrase one into a better-sounding version, because a claim that drifts is a claim nobody can check. The list is closed — a sentence that isn't on it doesn't appear as a claim anywhere, on the website or in the app. And only the company's owner can add to it or change it, never the person writing the page, and never as a side effect of writing one.

Everything else on a page is either a plain factual description of how something works, or a fact from the table below with a source behind it.

If a sentence isn't on the list, we don't say it.

Sourcing

Where the facts on a page come from

Each kind of fact has one source, one check, and one thing that makes us look at it again. Nothing here is on a calendar — a date on a SalamStay page moves when the fact was actually re-checked, not when the month changed.

How each kind of fact is sourced and re-checked
Kind of fact Source How it is checked What triggers a re-check
City and area facts A maintained city-facts record held alongside the page — named areas, markets, roads, masjids and distances. Every named place is checked against a primary local source before the page publishes. Anything still unresolved is left out of the page, not published with a hedge. The place changes, the page is edited, or a reader tells us it's wrong.
Prices and fees The live booking system. Read from live data at render time. While a page has no live listings behind it, it shows a placeholder — never a number typed into the page to make it look finished. Continuously, from the system. Prices are never hand-written into editorial copy.
Verification and document rules The product itself — the screens that actually ask for the document. The page must match what the app asks for, including which party type triggers which document and who can see it. The flow changes.
Regulation and licensing The authority that sets the rule. The authority is named in full on the page so you can check it yourself — for example ICT Police for Islamabad, and Punjab Police — Hotel Eye for Punjab. Where we don't yet have a named authority for a city, we say the rule is set by local authorities and name no one. The authority changes the rule, or a reader reports a mismatch.
Details of one home The host. Shown as the host's own statement about their home, and labelled that way. We check it against what the host uploaded, not against our own visit. The host edits their listing.
Reviews Guests and hosts who completed a stay. Published as written, both directions. We do not summarise them into a score that does not exist, and a home with no reviews is shown as new rather than rated. A new review is left, or one is removed under the community standards.
We are in beta, and the sourcing rules are stricter because of it SalamStay is live in six cities. There are no guest counts, host counts, ratings or awards to report yet, so none appear anywhere — and a page about a place we don't operate in doesn't get written to attract search traffic. Fact-checking

How a page is checked before it publishes

The same five passes run on every editorial page, in this order.

  1. Claims are matched against the closed list Every sentence that reads like a claim is compared with the nine, word for word. A near-miss is not accepted as a match — it is cut, or rewritten as a plain description of what the product does.
  2. Every named fact is traced to its source Places, distances, documents, authorities, dates. The trace is recorded with the page, so the next person editing it can see where a fact came from instead of guessing. A fact that can't be traced is removed — the page ships shorter.
  3. Numbers that move are wired, not typed Prices, availability and fees are read from the system. If a page needs a number the system can't give it yet, the page shows a placeholder and says what it is waiting for.
  4. A person verifies anything software helped write We use software in drafting. Where we do, a person checks every fact in the draft against its source before it publishes, and the page carries that check the same way any other page does. A draft nobody verified does not go out.
  5. The page is dated with the day it was reviewed Not the day it was touched. If we fix a typo, the review date stays where it was; if we re-check the facts, it moves. That way an old date on a SalamStay page means the facts are old — which is information you can use.

Limits

What we don't publish

These are not preferences. They are the things a SalamStay page is not permitted to contain, in either language.

  • Numbers we don't haveNo counts of guests, hosts or bookings, no ratings, no awards, no testimonials. Pre-launch, all of them would be invented.
  • SuperlativesNo ranking of ourselves against anyone, and no comparison we haven't done the work to support.
  • Religious authoritySalamStay is not a religious authority and does not certify compliance — we build features that respect how our guests live. No page may imply otherwise.
  • Pages written for a search engineNo near-identical page per city with the place names swapped, and no page about a city where you can't actually book. If there is nothing locally true to say, the page isn't written.
  • Paid placement dressed as editorialNothing on a SalamStay page is there because someone paid for it to be there. If that ever changes, it will be labelled on the page, plainly.
  • Fear as marketingCultural and safety features are stated as plain facts at the same weight as any other feature — never sold by making you afraid of the alternative.

Accountability

Who is accountable for what you read

Editorial pages — help articles, the local facts on city and area pages, the trust pages, and guides — are published by SalamStay Editorial, which is the accountable publisher for them. Its profile page says what it covers, and it is the byline you will see where a page has no personal author.

We do not put a person's name on a page they did not write. Where a guide is written by someone with genuine first-hand knowledge of a city, that guide carries their name and their profile page is published alongside it, saying who they are and what they actually know at first hand. No guide currently carries a personal byline — so no name appears on one, and none is invented in the meantime.

If you find something wrong, that is a correction, and corrections have their own policy: what counts as one, how to report it, and the log of every correction we've published.
