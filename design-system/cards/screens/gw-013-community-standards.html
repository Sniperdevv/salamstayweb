Community standards

What is and isn't acceptable on SalamStay, for guests and hosts alike — written plainly, applied to both sides of a booking. Short, because there isn't much to say: be accurate, be respectful, and keep the home the way you found it.

Section 1

How to read these

These standards apply to both sides of a booking. A host doesn't get a longer leash than a guest, and a guest doesn't get one over a host. Where a standard names one side, it's because only that side can do the thing described.

They are also short on purpose. You are trusted by default — we don't ask you to prove anything about yourself before you've done anything, and we don't write a rule for a problem we haven't had.

SalamStay is not a religious authority and does not certify compliance — we build features that respect how our guests live. This applies to every standard below. Where a home is described as no-alcohol, halal-kitchen or women-only, that is the host's stated policy for their home, shown as a fact you can read and act on — not a judgement SalamStay makes about anyone.

Section 2

Respect for the home

Every home carries its host's house rules. They cover the practical things — when you can arrive, when the neighbours sleep, whether visitors can stay over. You'll agree to these rules once, at booking review, before you pay, so nothing in them is a surprise on arrival.

What the standards themselves require

  • Keep to the arrival and departure timesCheck-in and check-out windows are on every listing — a typical one is check-in 2:00 PM–8:00 PM and check-out before 11:00 AM. Message the host if you'll arrive later.
  • Keep quiet hoursWhere the listing sets them — often 10:00 PM–6:00 AM. Many homes are in family buildings; keep music and gatherings low at night.
  • Bring only the people on the bookingDaytime visitors are welcome where the host allows them — let the host know if guests will stay overnight. No parties or events where the building doesn't allow large gatherings.
  • Follow the smoking and pet rules on the listingMany homes are no-smoking inside, with a balcony available, and pet-free for allergies. What the listing says is what applies.
Separate — what the host asks Some hosts add their own requests for their home. These are the host's preferences, stated by them and shown on the listing before you book — please read them the same way as the rest of the house rules. They are not SalamStay rules, and SalamStay does not assess or certify them.
  • No alcohol on the premises"This is a no-alcohol home."
  • Kitchen is kept halal"Please don't bring pork into the home."
  • Prayer mat & marked Qibla"In the main bedroom, there if you'd like them."
  • Shoes off inside, please"There's a rack by the front door."

Section 3

Alcohol and what a listing states

No-alcohol listings by default. Hosts who allow alcohol must explicitly opt in and disclose it. That means the default position on SalamStay is stated for you, and any departure from it is a fact printed on the listing — never something you have to ask about or guess at.

The standard here is about honesty, not observance: a listing must state what is actually true of the home, and a guest must keep to what the listing says. A host who allows alcohol and hides it, and a guest who ignores a no-alcohol home, break the same standard.

The same logic covers the other attributes a host can state — Halal-kitchen, prayer-space, and Qibla direction shown on listings, and Women-only stays hosted by women. These are options some hosts set for their home, stated on the listing as host policy — never assumed for you, never applied to your account.

Section 4

Photographs

Listing photos have to do two jobs at once: show a guest what they're actually booking, and protect the privacy of everyone who lives in or near the home. Hosts commit to pardah-respectful photography — modest, respectful photos throughout the listing.

  • No people in frameFamily members and staff stay out of shot — never identify anyone by including them, even in the background.
  • Family-space discretionPersonal or family areas — like a women-only floor — are shown empty and tidy, never candid.
  • Windows framed away from neighboursAngle shots so a window doesn't look directly into a neighbouring home.
  • Show what guests will actually findWide shots from a corner, not close-ups. Skip filters — the photo should match the home on arrival.

These are practical and dignified, not a checklist to pass — they're the same considerations any thoughtful host already applies.

Standard not yet written What a guest may photograph inside a home The rules above are what a host commits to when photographing their own listing. SalamStay has not yet written the guest-side standard — whether and how a guest may photograph a home they are staying in, and the people in it. Until it exists, ask your host, and treat anyone else's presence as private.

Section 5

Respect between people

These are the behaviours SalamStay acts on. They apply in messages, on profiles, and in the home — and they apply identically to guests and hosts.

  • Harassment or bullyingThreatening, abusive, or repeated unwanted contact.
  • Inappropriate messagesMessages that are offensive or make you uncomfortable.
  • Suspicious behaviourSomething feels off — a possible scam or fake account.
  • Safety concernsAnything that makes you worry about your safety, or someone else's.

If something doesn't fit any of these, tell us in your own words. The list is how we sort reports, not a limit on what you can raise.

Section 6

Listings must be accurate

A listing is a promise about a real place. These are the ways that promise gets broken, and each is something we act on.

  • Inaccurate listingPhotos, location or details don't match the real place.
  • Safety concernSomething here could put a guest at risk.
  • Breaks SalamStay policyDiscrimination, prohibited items, or off-platform requests.
  • Offensive contentText or photos that aren't appropriate.
  • Possible scamAsked to pay or message outside SalamStay.
  • Attributes must be trueLoad-shedding hours, backup power, halal kitchen, prayer space, Qibla and women-only are facts about the home. Listings show load-shedding hours and backup power — stating them wrongly is the same breach as a wrong photo.

Section 7

Reporting and blocking

These are two separate tools, and you never have to justify using either one.

  • Report a listing or a personYour report goes to our safety team. Reporting is private, and it helps keep SalamStay safe for everyone — the person you report is not told who reported them. A person on our safety team reads every report.
  • Block someoneBlocking sets a clear boundary — it hides, it doesn't accuse. The blocked person is never notified. It's reversible at any time from your blocked-people list.

Exactly what blocking does

  • You can't message each otherYour conversation closes, both ways.
  • Neither of you can book the otherThey can't request your listings, and you can't book theirs.
  • You won't see each otherYou'll disappear from each other's profiles and search.
  • They are not notifiedBlocking is private. The person is never told you blocked them.

Section 8

When you and a host disagree

Not every problem is a breach of these standards. Sometimes a guest and a host simply see a stay differently and need help resolving it. That is what mediation is for — not a complaint filed against your host, and not a case you win.

  1. 1 Case openedYou describe what happened, scoped to one booking, and attach anything that helps.
  2. 2 Both sides share their viewYour host is asked for their side too. Symmetric respect — neither account is treated as the default truth.
  3. 3 SalamStay mediatesOur team reviews both statements. The amanah hold stays in place until the case resolves.
  4. 4 ResolutionThe outcome is stated plainly — what is returned, to whom, and when — with no editorialising about who was right.

Section 9

If a standard is broken

We'd rather be straight with you about what SalamStay can actually do today than describe a system we haven't built. Two things can currently interrupt an account or a listing, and neither of them is a conduct penalty:

  • An account hold, while a payment dispute is resolvedNothing here is permanent — let's get it resolved together. You keep your trips, receipts and support; new bookings and messaging pause. You can appeal, and a person on our team reads every appeal.
  • A listing pause, while a licence is renewedPer-listing and reversible: confirmed bookings are honoured, the calendar and photos are kept, and it comes back the moment the renewal verifies.

Beyond those, a report goes to the safety team and a person reviews it. We don't state a reply time we can't keep, so none is stated.

Standard not yet written The conduct-enforcement ladder, and appeal rights against it What follows a substantiated report — warning, suspension, removal — how each step is decided, how long it lasts, and how you appeal it. No conduct-enforcement ladder exists on SalamStay today, so none is described here. Publishing one before it is built would claim a power we have not defined, and promise you an appeal route that has nowhere to go.
