Screen — Trip safety setup (GA-089)

The four-step walkthrough of the settings GA-125 already ships, at /account/settings/privacy/trip-safety/setup, reached from the GA-087 hub. Naming follows shipped doctrine, not the registry row: GA-125 labels this surface "Trip safety" and GA-091 refuses the "women's mode" framing outright — so neither script carries that phrase, and the §2 row title is parked for the founder. Steps: who can see and message you · where you appear on a map · what you'd like to see first when you search · arriving somewhere new, then a plain review. Every step ends with the same two-line block — what you control and what is off unless you turn it on — so nothing is quietly enabled on a guest's behalf. Progress is a thin four-bar sub-progress for this setup only; it is deliberately not the four-step Party → Verify → Price → Confirm booking stepper, and its caption never borrows that journey's wording (§7.6a). Steps 1 and 2 reproduce GA-125's shipped rows rather than restating them — the two radiogroups with their "Safer default" chips, the approximate-location Switch on by default, and the blocked-people row whose hint carries GA-133's load-bearing truth verbatim: "2 people · they're never notified." Step 3 is the one that could go wrong, so it is written narrowly: a women-only home is a policy a host sets for their own home (GW-006's shipped sentence), and this chip only changes the order of the guest's own results. The screen says all three limits out loud — it sorts, it doesn't hide; hosts never see it; nothing is chosen for the guest — because a "safety" setting that silently narrows someone's options is not a safety setting. Nowhere does the wizard ask a guest to declare a gender, a vulnerability, or a mode. Step 4 reuses GA-091's Switch verbatim — label, subtitle, and its off-by-default state — and repeats that screen's boundary word for word: shares your arrival, not your live location. The check-in switch is on because check-in is an offer the guest can decline in the moment; the sharing switch is off because it involves someone else.
