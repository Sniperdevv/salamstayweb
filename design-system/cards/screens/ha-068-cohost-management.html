The matrix is a real <table> — <caption>, th scope="col", th scope="row" — so a screen-reader user hears "Payouts and bank details · Co-host · Never" as one coherent cell, and the Next.js build binds row-for-row. Three permissions are delegable (calendar, guest messages, nightly pricing) and are shown twice: as switches at invite time, and as chips on the person's row so the current state is legible without opening anything. The two owner-only rows carry the same visual weight as every other row — no red, no warning tint — and their explanation is a statement about the account ("bound to the verified account holder"), never about the co-host. In the invite sheet they appear as visible, dashed, locked switches rather than being hidden: the host can see the boundary exists and why, which is the GA-075 informative-disabled convention. Access is scoped per home: Ali covers two of Aqib's three, Sana covers one. A pending invite shares nothing until it is accepted, and "Remove co-host" is the single destructive-token use on the surface.

Mirrored end to end — the back and row chevrons flip, the table's text-align:start carries every heading and cell to the reading edge without a second stylesheet, and the permission chips unstack from the right. Names use the two-letter monogram convention (GA-096): علی → عل, بلال → بل. Dates and counts are Western digits inside .num, LTR-isolated, with Urdu prose around them, and the Urdu lexicon holds — شناخت is identity/verify, never تصدیق (Pass #31 ruling). The Urdu security note repeats the English one word for word in intent: a fact about the account, ending with "کسی پر شک نہیں" — no suspicion of anyone.
