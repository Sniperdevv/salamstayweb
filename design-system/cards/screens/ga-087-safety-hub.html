Screen — Safety hub (GA-087)

The calm-at-rest safety area at /safety, reached from the trip detail's safety toolkit (GA-071) and the post-registration rows (GA-077/GA-078). Five entries, all plain rows: Emergency (GA-092) · Trip safety (GA-089/GA-125) · Trusted contacts (GA-090) · Arrival sharing (GA-091) · Safety resources. Naming follows shipped doctrine, not the registry row: GA-091 and GA-125 both surface this as "Trip safety" — privacy defaults that help on any trip — and never as a "women's-safety mode"; that framing is carried here in both scripts, and the §2 row title is parked for the founder rather than propagated. Emergency is calm at rest: a brand-outlined row, never a red panic surface — the muted error tone appears only at the moment of use, inside GA-092 (§9-G, §10.8). The area is deliberately light so it loads instantly on a poor connection, and it stays usable offline. The whole surface is plain rows and one outlined entry — no siren red, no alarm iconography, no fear language anywhere (§10.8). Emergency is visually first and visually calm: brand outline over interactive.subtle, a shield glyph, and a caption promising a confirm step, so nothing about the resting state suggests panic. Naming is the shipped doctrine, not the §2 row: GA-125 ships this entry as "Trip safety" with the hint "privacy defaults that help on any trip," and GA-091 refuses the "women's mode" label outright — both carried here verbatim, so the surface never asks a woman to identify herself as needing protection to get it. Shipped hints are reused rather than rewritten: "they're never notified" (GA-125/GA-133), "they only see what you choose to share" (GA-090), "shares your arrival, not your live location" (GA-091), "reporting is private" (GA-132). The first-run state (B) never reads as broken: the defaults are already on, and the two unset rows name their own next action with a neutral chip rather than a warning (§8.5, no dead ends). The foot line carries GA-109's honesty — a person reads every ticket, no response-time promise.

Dark carries the identical token set, AA-proven (DESIGN §1.6): the emergency entry keeps its brand outline rather than gaining a glow, and the "On" chip stays the muted success register in both themes. Offline is a state, not a failure — the banner sits in bg.raised with a wifi-off glyph, never semantic.error, and the copy is specific about what still works: emergency calls use the phone's own dialler and need no data (§9-G perf: emergency degrades to a native dial intent), saved contact numbers are already on the device, and settings sync themselves.
